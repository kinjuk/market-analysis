03 Flags to Revisit
DDM and Lynch Structurally Understate ASML DDM (€560) and Lynch (€570) both anchor on earnings and dividends in a way that penalises ASML's unique monopoly structure. ASML's 0.59% yield makes it a growth compounder, not an income vehicle. Lynch's formula (EPS × growth) applies a 20% growth rate to EPS — it rewards linear compounders and misses the step-change dynamic of EUV backlog monetisation. Strip these two out and the 3-model average of DCF, Multiples and Graham is €1,111.57 — 12.2% below current, still showing overvaluation but far less extreme.
ASML Trades at a Premium to Its Own Peer Group ASML's current forward P/E of 44.4x compares to a peer median of 37.8x. That 6.6x premium is the market's EUV monopoly tax — and it may be justified. But at €1,265, the premium is already priced. Any deceleration in backlog conversion or pushback in customer capex timelines compresses the multiple quickly. The 52-week range of €587.80 to €1,344.40 illustrates exactly how violently the market reprices ASML on expectation shifts.
China Export Restrictions — Structural Revenue Risk ASML's China revenue was approximately 29% of total in 2023 before restrictions tightened. US and Dutch export controls have progressively limited shipment of EUV and advanced DUV systems to Chinese customers. This is not a temporary risk — it is a permanent structural reduction in ASML's addressable market. Management has guided for lower China revenue as a percentage of mix going forward. The long-term bull case requires non-China demand (TSMC Arizona, Samsung, Intel) to fully absorb the gap.
FCF Volatility — Capex Cycle Timing ASML's FCF is highly lumpy: −26.3% in 2022, +63.9% in 2025E. This reflects the timing of system deliveries rather than underlying business deterioration. The DCF uses €10B as the 2025E base — management guided for €10B+ FCF in their 2025 outlook. If deliveries slip into 2026, FCF compresses and the DCF output drops materially.
Average · Weighting The 3-model average of DCF, Multiples and Graham (€1,111.57) is the most intellectually defensible output for a capital equipment monopoly. DDM and Lynch are included for framework consistency but should carry lower weight in any investment decision. At €1,265, ASML is approximately 12% above even the most generous defensible estimate.
