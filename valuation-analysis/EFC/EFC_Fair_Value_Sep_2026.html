03 Flags to Revisit
Book Value Erosion — The Central Risk EFC's book value per share dropped from $18.45 (Dec 2025) to $13.61 (Jun 2026) — a 26.2% decline in six months. This is not a rounding error. It reflects mark-to-market losses on the mortgage portfolio driven by rate movements, spread widening, or credit deterioration. Until the source of this erosion is understood and stabilised, every model output that uses current BV as an anchor is vulnerable to further downward revision. This is the single most important fact in this report.
EFC Trades at a Premium to Peer Median P/Book EFC's P/Book of 0.974x is above the peer median of 0.866x. Given that BV has fallen 26.2% in six months, paying close to book for EFC requires confidence that BV has stabilised. RITM (0.826x) and TWO (0.793x est.) are pricing in more uncertainty. AGNC's premium (1.235x) reflects its pure-agency status and government-backed portfolio — not comparable to EFC's hybrid credit book.
ADE DCF and Graham Are Optimistic Anchors ADE DCF ($21.01) and Graham ($20.79) both produce outputs well above current price because they capitalise ADE earnings power without penalising BV deterioration. If BV continues to erode, ADE will eventually follow as the portfolio shrinks. These models are the bull case ceiling, not the central estimate.
Dividend Coverage Is Holding — For Now Q4 2025 ADE of $0.47 covered the $0.39 quarterly dividend 1.21x, marking six consecutive quarters of coverage. Moody's revised the outlook to Positive and affirmed Ba3. That's meaningful. But ADE coverage is an earnings story; BV erosion is a balance sheet story. Both matter. Six quarters of coverage does not protect against BV-driven dilution if the portfolio continues to reprice.
Two-Model mREIT Approach Puts Fair Value Near Current Price The purist mREIT approach — DDM + P/Book — averages $12.98, only 2.1% below current. This is the most grounded estimate for an mREIT: income value via DDM, balance sheet value via P/Book. It says EFC is approximately fairly valued at $13.26, not materially discounted. The 5-model average of $16.27 is pulled up by the DCF and Graham outputs, which are optimistic on earnings without reflecting BV risk. Analyst consensus of $14.86 (+13.96%) sits between the two approaches — a reasonable midpoint.
Beta 0.93 — Rate Sensitive, Not Volatile EFC's beta of 0.93 reflects moderate market correlation — less volatile than pure-equity plays but meaningfully rate-sensitive. A 100bps move in the 10-year Treasury will reprice the entire mortgage portfolio and BV simultaneously. The current 11.76% yield compensates for this risk, but only if BV stops deteriorating. At current price, you are being paid to wait — the question is whether the wait ends in recovery or further erosion.
