03 Flags to Revisit
Two Approaches — Two Conclusions That Agree The 5-model average ($15.62) and the 2-model BDC-purist average ($15.43) are within $0.19 of each other. That convergence is meaningful — it suggests the adapted models (NII DCF, Graham, Yield) are not distorting the output. Both approaches point to the same thesis: OBDC is undervalued by ~27% at current prices.
NII Headwind — Rate Cuts OBDC's loan book is predominantly floating rate. As the Fed cuts rates, NII per share compresses directly. NII fell from $1.98 in 2023 to $1.59 in 2025 — a 20% decline — and further cuts could push it lower. The DCF accounts for this with a −2% projection in years 1–2, but the pace of cuts remains the central risk to the thesis.
Graham Adaptation — Treat as Cross-Check Only Graham's formula was built for operating companies. Substituting NII for EPS is defensible but not native. The $16.47 output should be read as a soft upper bound on intrinsic value, not a primary target.
NAV Erosion Risk NAV/share has declined from $15.47 (Q1 2025) to $14.81 (Q4 2025) — a 4.3% drop driven by credit markdowns on a small number of names. Non-accruals at 2.3% of cost (1.1% of fair value) are manageable but rising. If credit deterioration accelerates, NAV compresses further and the P/NAV model fair value falls with it.
External Management Fee Drag OBDC is externally managed by Blue Owl Credit. Management and incentive fees reduce net income available to shareholders. This is already reflected in NII figures, but is worth noting as a structural disadvantage vs internally managed BDCs like MAIN, which command premium P/NAV multiples as a result.
