03 Flags to Revisit
Capex Cycle Distorts FCF — Use Revenue DCF Oracle's reported FCF was −$23.7B in FY2026 due to cloud infrastructure capex of approximately $30B. This is not a business deteriorating — it's a business investing aggressively in the AI cloud buildout. The Revenue DCF ($188.99) normalises for this by projecting revenue and applying a 25% net margin at maturity. When capex normalises (management targets FCF positive from FY2027E), the FCF story recovers fast. But at $158, you are buying before that recovery is visible in the numbers.
ORCL Trades at a Premium to Peer Median on Forward P/E Oracle's current fwd P/E of 20.9x is above the peer median of 17.22x (MSFT, SAP, CRM). The multiples model therefore shows overvaluation at $130.87. The premium is driven by the market pricing in Oracle's AI infrastructure contracts — $7B DoD deal, hyperscaler partnerships — ahead of earnings delivery. Whether that premium is justified depends on FY2027 execution. The analyst consensus of $265 implies a 25x+ fwd P/E — reflecting confidence that Oracle deserves the premium. At 17x, it should be closer to $130. The current price at 21x is a bet on execution, not a value play.
Net Debt $78B — Leverage Is the Structural Risk Oracle carries approximately $88B in gross debt and ~$10B cash, giving net debt of ~$78B. Net Debt/EBITDA of ~4.2x is high for a software company. Interest coverage of 4.77x is adequate but not comfortable. The debt was largely taken on to fund buybacks and acquisitions (Cerner). As long as cloud revenue keeps growing at 20%+ and capex normalises, the leverage is manageable. If cloud growth disappoints, the debt becomes the first thing the market reprices.
DDM Spread Is Too Tight to Be Reliable The DDM uses r=11%, g=10%, giving a 1% spread and FV $220. At g=9%, FV drops to $165. At g=11%, the model is undefined (g = r). For a company where dividend growth has averaged 13.4% over 6 years, using 10% is already conservative — but the tight spread means small assumption changes move the output dramatically. The DDM is included for completeness; it's not the right primary framework for Oracle.
Lynch Flags the PEG at 1.39 — Not Cheap on Growth Lynch FV of $114 puts Oracle 28% below current price. The PEG of 1.39x (P/E 20.9x vs 15% growth) means you're paying $1.39 for every unit of growth — not expensive by tech standards, but not a bargain either. At the 52-week low of $114.51, Oracle was at Lynch FV. The current price implies the market has repriced Oracle from "distressed software" to "AI infrastructure compounder" — a 38% re-rating in three months (from $114 to $158).
