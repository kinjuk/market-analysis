03 Flags to Revisit
Lynch · Model Drag Lynch's FV = EPS × growth rate produces $25.20 — well below every other model — because VZ's 5% growth rate is modest by design. VZ is a yield vehicle and a value play, not a growth story. Lynch's formula penalises low-growth compounders structurally. Include as a floor, not a target.
Frontier Acquisition Leverage The Frontier acquisition added ~$20B in debt, bringing net debt to ~$143B. This is the central risk in the thesis. The DCF explicitly deducts net debt from enterprise value. If FCF recovery is slower than the guided $21.5B+, the equity value compresses faster than it looks from the headline numbers. Debt/EBITDA of ~2.8x is manageable but leaves little room for error.
DDM · Near Fair Value The DDM output of $48.35 is almost exactly the current price of $47.57 — within 1.6%. For a model that uses a 22-year dividend growth streak and a conservative 2.5% growth rate, this is meaningful. VZ is neither deeply undervalued nor overvalued on a pure dividend basis. The margin is thin.
Multiples · TMUS Premium T-Mobile's 19.8x fwd P/E reflects superior subscriber growth and a stronger balance sheet — it is not a natural comp for VZ's value/income profile. Including it pulls the median up from AT&T's 11.3x to a blended 11.3x (median unchanged in this case, but worth noting). If TMUS is excluded, the peer median drops to 9.4x, fair value on multiples falls to $47.38 — essentially at market.
Average · Weighting Graham ($77.26) pulls the average up meaningfully. VZ's 5% EPS growth is conservative but Graham's formula amplifies it. The three most market-grounded models — DDM ($48), DCF ($60), Multiples ($57) — average $55, which is a cleaner central estimate for VZ than the full 5-model average of $53.
